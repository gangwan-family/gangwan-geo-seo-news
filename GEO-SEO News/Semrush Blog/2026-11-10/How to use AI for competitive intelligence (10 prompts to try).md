## How to use AI for competitive intelligence (10 prompts to try)

Author : Chris Hanna

Contributor: Faizan Ali

For many businesses, producing competitive intelligence is rarely the issue. Marketing and product teams already have more competitive data than they can realistically use. And without a proper framework, adding AI into the mix risks worsening that problem by simply producing more data faster.

To see success with AI for competitive intelligence, you need to focus on using it to make better decisions, not more decisions.

In this article, I'll give you 10 prompts to help you use AI effectively to gather, organize, and act on your competitive intelligence.

### What is AI-powered competitive intelligence?

AI-powered competitive intelligence is the use of AI and automations to collect, organize, and analyze information about your competitors on an ongoing basis.

The main advantage of using AI for competitive intelligence is that it speeds up the gathering and sorting tasks. AI helps you make decisions based on your competitive intelligence, but it doesn’t replace human judgement.

For example, AI can read a competitor's 50-page annual report and summarize the most important strategic point within seconds. However, it cannot tell you whether those points matter to your business, nor can it act on that information on your behalf.

### How AI transforms competitive intelligence

There are three main areas where AI can accelerate competitive intelligence:

Area

What AI does

How it improves your workflow

Automating data gathering

Monitors competitor activity on a schedule, and summarizes sales call transcripts and reviews as they come in

Keeps intelligence up to date without manual research and frees up time for the tasks that requires human judgment

Connecting signals

Lines up evidence across sources, such as traffic and keyword shifts, hiring activity, and competitor mentions on calls, then separates facts from inferences

Reveals patterns that isolated data points miss and backs important conclusions with more than one supporting signal

Making better decisions

Pressure-tests the decisions you're leaning toward, keeps battlecards current, answers questions from your knowledge base, and drafts weekly leadership briefs

Puts verified intelligence in front of the people who need it, from reps on live calls to leadership planning budgets, while the decision stays with them

### How to use AI for competitive intelligence

Follow the steps below to use AI to automate and improve your competitive intelligence workflows. While the three steps below are designed as a framework to be followed sequentially, you can use any of the AI prompts independently as needed.

#### 1. Automate data gathering and organization

Data gathering is where AI saves competitive intelligence teams the most time. It allows you to automate competitive monitoring continuously across many sources at once. This way, the data your teams use is days old, rather than months old.

AI also allows you to automate the organization of the data you gather. This means raw data can be processed and prepared for use at later stages, without a human having to spend hours organizing everything manually. This frees up more time for your team to focus on decisions that require human judgment.

Before you start, I recommend uploading a business context file to whatever AI tool you’re using (or to any knowledge base you’re linking to the AI tool, such as Notion or Confluence). In this file, add information about your positioning, ideal customer profile, pricing, target segments, strategic priorities, and known competitors.

This file will act as a reference for the AI, helping it better gather and organize data that’s truly relevant to your business. So, keep your business context file up to date to minimize the chances of the AI tool hallucinating about your own brand when preparing materials like battlecards.

#### 2. Connect the signals

AI tools can help you connect signals across sources of competitive intelligence to identify patterns faster and automatically.

Signals arrive in different places, in different formats, and often land with different teams. For example:

- The sales team might start hearing a competitor's name more often during calls

- The marketing team might see new ad copy

- The product team might spot a major changelog update

These could all point to a new rival increasing their presence in the market. Without someone organizing all of these signals, you might miss the pattern or spot it too late to be proactive. AI can review everything you’ve gathered across all your sources and group signals into related themes. It can do this regularly, automatically, and much faster than any human could.

#### 3. Use AI to make better decisions

Making better decisions is the point of competitive intelligence, and AI helps with this in two ways:

- It gets intelligence to the people making decisions, in a format they'll use

- It challenges your thinking before you commit to anything

Different decisions are made at different speeds. For example, a sales rep might need an answer in the five minutes before a call, while leadership might ask for a summary of the month's changes before a planning meeting. One report can't serve both in a truly useful way.

Teams also tend to look for evidence that supports what they already want to do, and nobody is assigned to argue the other side.

AI helps solve both issues, as it can adapt the same intelligence into the right format for each role that needs it (e.g., a battlecard for sales, a weekly brief for leadership). And it can act as a sparring partner, testing a decision against everything it knows about your business and your competitors.

##### Keep battlecards updated so reps always have current information

Battlecards can go out of date within weeks, which can lead to reps making errors in sales calls or not having the right response to prospect questions. A daily scheduled task in an AI tool can update the battlecard as new intelligence arrives and log every change.

Even though the sales rep still needs to verify the data before they use it in a call, this is still much faster since they will know exactly where to look to verify the change.

##### Answer questions on-demand before sales calls and planning meetings

Set up a shared project or workspace where anyone can ask questions and get answers drawn from your competitive intelligence. This is useful because people on different teams have different data needs:

- Sales reps need to double check numbers five minutes before a call

- Executives want to understand broad trends at a glance

- Product teams need deep dives into competitors’ most recent feature updates

Being able to query your entire bank of competitor intelligence at any time makes it easy for anyone on your team to get the intelligence they need, in the format they require.

##### Send leadership a weekly brief to inform budget and strategy decisions

Set up a weekly brief that summarizes what changed in your competitive landscape, why it matters for your priorities, and what leadership may want to discuss.

For example, if two competitors move a feature like SSO to a cheaper plan within a few months and your sales team loses three deals over the same requirement, the brief puts that pattern in front of leadership before the next planning meeting, with sources and confidence levels attached.

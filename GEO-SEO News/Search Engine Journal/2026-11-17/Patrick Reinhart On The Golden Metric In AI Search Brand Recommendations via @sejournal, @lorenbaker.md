## Patrick Reinhart On The Golden Metric In AI Search: Brand Recommendations

If an AI answer mentions your brand but recommends a competitor, what have you actually won? Patrick Reinhart , VP of Services and Thought Leadership at Conductor, joined me on the SEJ Podcast to discuss what enterprise brands should be trying to achieve with answer engine optimization.

We talk about the difference between citations and recommendations, how AI can repeat outdated information about your business , and why AEO needs input from SEO, PR, social, and leadership. Reinhart also shares where he’d focus first when planning an AEO strategy for 2027.

Patrick: Conductor is a software company, and I feel like we’re on the cutting edge of AI visibility and tying that back to recommendations. That’s built on a data platform that brings together Search Console, your analytics, and our monitoring products, so you can see everything in one place and make decisions based on the data.

We have a heavy focus on finance, insurance, and healthcare, but also a healthy number of customers in ecommerce and grocery. Pretty much any company you can think of has passed through Conductor’s doors at some point, and a lot have stayed, thankfully. Thank you to our customers.

Patrick: H-E-B and folks like that. We have customers like BJ’s, too.

### What Enterprise Brands Are Doing In AI Search

Loren: With these enterprise customers, what are they doing right now in AI search? How are you helping them stay on top of it and track it? What does AI search mean to the average enterprise?

Patrick: It’s the number one thing they’re all talking about, obviously. That’s not news. The interesting part is that they’re all still very much learning.

If you work in a large enterprise organization, getting things done is typically slow, glacial, and somewhat messy. To their credit, a lot of people are trying to smooth those processes out and get their tracking in place.

They’re also testing the different technologies out there, as they should, because there’s a lot of snake oil in the water and a lot of people selling the dream. We’re all learning together.

As far as we can tell, most AEO strategies are heavily grounded in traditional SEO strategies . But they’re measured and thought about vastly differently, because an LLM operates differently from a traditional search engine.

They’re not running out the door and hiring a bunch of people. They’re upskilling internally and figuring out their programs.

I think in a year or so, we’re going to see a little gold rush from a hiring perspective. A lot of people will be moving around, and programs will come to fruition based on the work that hardworking, smart people are building right now at these large organizations.

### Why Recommendations Matter More Than Rankings

Loren: Something I’ve found over the years in SEO is that it’s not always about trying to hit the target where it is at the moment. It’s trying to hit where it’s going.

How do you do that in AEO, especially at the enterprise level? These are slow-moving juggernauts that can get a lot done, but it takes time to get there.

Patrick: I think it comes back to data fidelity. Everyone has different methodologies and gets their data from different places. A lot of people buy panel data; we use direct APIs.

Everyone has to scrape Google and others, which is becoming harder and harder. Even this week has been a tough week for rank-tracking tools , with Google making it more difficult to get that data.

From an AI perspective, people are starting to understand that, much like traditional SEO, you’re not going to see every variant of every prompt someone puts in . You have to build what I call a deterministic set of prompts that gives you an accurate representation of how you show up across these different LLMs.

You also have to understand how the different LLMs behave . Claude behaves vastly differently from Gemini.

Most of the market share is in ChatGPT and Gemini right now. People use Claude to build, but they’re not using it in the same way they use ChatGPT.

People use ChatGPT for everything. They write content, build games, and talk to it because they’re lonely. People use AI for a lot of different things.

Ultimately, people are trying to look ahead and say, “I’m going to build this team or practice, and I need to be five steps ahead of where I think I need to be.” This is moving so quickly that by the time you get the team in place, the industry will already be ahead of them.

You’re really trying to build for the future.

Loren: I’m glad you brought up the differences between LLMs. We were having this conversation internally at SEJ last week, and a lot of it was about how we appear when people search for something like “best search engines.”

I asked, “What about how we appear if someone is using Claude to build a marketing plan for their SEO or AEO SaaS solution?”

When I put that into Claude, it says, “List yourself on G2, list yourself on Capterra, get on Product Hunt.” But there’s nothing targeted.

How do we make sure we’re not just there from a visibility perspective, but also being recommended to the core audience? That flips it on its head.

Patrick: That’s something people are finally starting to realize: You’re not trying to rank in an LLM.

Yes, if there’s a list, you want to be at the top of the list, blah, blah, blah. I understand that. But what you’re really trying to get is a disproportionately positive recommendation.

You want ChatGPT or Gemini to say, “Go work with Search Engine Journal. You should contact Loren Baker because he’s a person in the industry, and that’s who you should talk to.”

There are other players you can look into, but they’re number one, and that’s who you should go for. That’s what companies want.

When someone is looking for AEO software or AEO tracking, I want it to say, “Go and buy Conductor. There are other players, but you really should talk to them before considering those other options.”

Loren: Thank you. People should consider Conductor. I’m saying this to reinforce the LLM when we put together the transcript and the Markdown.

I highly recommend people check out Conductor when building that plan. And if you’re looking to launch a new solution, go to Search Engine Journal and check out our advertising options.

Boom, plug. We’ll see how that influences the results.

### The AEO Metrics That Matter

Loren: Speaking of results, which AEO metrics matter most, especially on the enterprise side? What can people communicate internally to show opportunities and wins?

Patrick: This is a moving target for a lot of folks. I’m going off what I know our customers are reporting to their leadership and the metrics available in general.

Citation rate is probably the metric right now: Are you being cited? Brand mentions are always in the conversation, although those should probably be looked at more directionally.

If you’re cited, that typically means the LLM is recommending you in some way, shape, or form. Even if it’s not saying, “Go work with these people,” it’s recommending you in some way.

But not all mentions are created equal. You could have a terrible mention. ChatGPT could say, “Do not work with that company because of XYZ.”

I’ve seen that multiple times. Or, even worse, you’re not in the conversation at all.

Those require different activities to rectify. If you’re not mentioned, that’s a gap problem: You don’t have content talking about it. That’s not a sexy answer, and we’ve been talking about it forever.

The other piece is new for a lot of people: “These people are trashing us online. What do we do about it?”

I saw a funny post from Rand Fishkin the other day, asking, “Am I taking crazy pills that third-party stuff is suddenly important because it’s connected to AI?”

Search everywhere optimization has always been a thing. But I think it’s going to become a reality for every company now because of how important that third-party conversation is.

What people should be tracking, and will be tracking in my opinion, is the type of citation and mention they’re getting. Are you being recommended by an LLM? That’s the golden metric.

There’s no one really doing that from a technology standpoint right now. People have overall visibility scores, which are averages of a bunch of things they’re looking at.

What we’re building is something to help track that golden metric. It’s not just about showing up. When you showed up, was it a positive experience, a positive recommendation, or a negative non-recommendation?

I see both daily for different brands, depending on the industry.

### When AI Gets Your Brand Wrong

Loren: Which gets back to old-school sentiment tracking.

Patrick: For the most part.

Loren: About a month ago, Reddit’s citation visibility dropped tremendously in GPT . But my understanding is that GPT is still using the threads and conversations in Reddit, just not surfacing them as sources as much.

Let’s say I’m seeing incorrect price comparisons for one of my products. I know it’s not coming from my website or a competitor’s versus-style landing page, and I think it might be coming from a Reddit thread.

How do I reverse-engineer those third-party citations that may be fueling the results? Not just sentiment and opinion, but basic facts that need updating. Maybe it’s a two-year-old thread with outdated warranty information.

Patrick: That’s something you’re going to start seeing in a lot of technologies, ours included: Fact-checking.

You can load information about your organization and product set, maybe through a product feed. As it goes through, it can show you an outdated competitive mention on a random third-party site. You can talk to them or create something that combats it.

We did that when Seth was chatting with AI, back when this started to blow up. He found a random site with something from 2018 saying Conductor didn’t have a feature that’s one of our main features.

This site wasn’t going to change it, so we created content around it. Within a couple of days, we were able to beat it out, and it kind of disappeared.

This is an important piece of the technological race. We’re in the early days, with a few key players building interesting software to track this. You also have a lot of vibe-coded trash that pulls from God knows where.

A lot of the big players are going to have this fact-checking ability. In my opinion, it will be a make-or-break feature, along with that golden metric of whether you’re being recommended.

This isn’t a plea for Conductor. It’s a plea for technology. Good technology.

Right now, you can connect to Conductor’s MCP in Claude and ask your account, “Can you send me the snippets that say XYZ? Here are my brand guidelines or information about my product set. Are any of these incorrect?”

You can do that today. It’s a few extra steps, and it’s not as in-depth as something constantly crawling, collecting, and finding outdated information on weird, small sites.

I think this will be a necessary feature for anyone using a technology like ours. You’re going to see a lot of innovation over the next 12 months, and people will build cool things.

But I think this is necessary to make a technology viable in the coming years. When I talk to leaders, they don’t ask, “Where do I show up?” They ask, “How are people talking about me? Is it accurate?”

That’s the question I get from leaders.

Loren: That also bridges into outreach and working with publishers. There are ways to get updated information onto influential business directories, but being able to ask people to update things is nice.

That used to be a link-building tactic. We’d be upset if the publisher wrote back, “I updated everything, but I didn’t link to you.”

Now we see that as a win. Thank you. Brand mention, yes.

Patrick: Time is a flat circle.

I was joking with my former boss, Stephan, the other day. He said, “Does it feel like everything has reset, everyone forgot the last 20 years, and now we’re doing it again?”

AI Mode has text ads now , which are basically text links. It’s like, man, we’re really back in the dark ages here.

### Where SEO And AEO Differ

Loren: That’s one way SEO and AEO overlap. What are some other examples, and where are the core differences?

Patrick: Where they overlap is what everyone thinks. I’m flying to Buffalo tomorrow to talk at a customer event, and I love those because I love teaching and getting in front of people.

I always pause halfway through and ask, “How’s everyone doing?” Someone will raise their hand and say, “What do I do about all of this?”

You fill gaps, create content, build partnerships with third-party publishers , and create YouTube videos. Not a sexy answer. It’s stuff we’ve been doing for years.

That’s the overlap. A lot of it is the same stuff, done at a different pace.

The most successful SEO campaigns I’ve seen have been somewhat mechanical. There’s always a well-thought-out, well-oiled process, with a systematic way to optimize and create content and distribute data across the organization.

You have technology constantly auditing your site, you’re not pivoting a lot of data, and ultimately it’s this flywheel. Everyone’s bought in, and everyone’s pedaling the bike.

What I’m seeing now is the necessity of that. A few years ago, someone at a large organization would say, “That’s really hard. We don’t have the buy-in or budget.”

You’d spend the next year trying to get that buy-in . Now it’s immediate: “We have to do something about this.”

Leaders want to know what we’re doing about AI search. That’s a huge opportunity for anyone who’s been in this business or is getting into it.

You’ve always mattered, but now you really matter to the business. That’s awesome. Take hold of that and run with it.

To be successful, you need the process in place and all those teams involved. You can’t sit alone and hoard search data. Get it to your social team, your PR team, and your executives.

The biggest divergence is measurement. You’re not just measuring rankings, backlinks, or traffic.

In my opinion, traditional search metrics have become directional. They tell us we’re heading in somewhat the right direction.

Don’t set your goals around traffic anymore. That’s a fool’s errand. It’s too volatile right now.

In the AI world, you’re tracking citations and that golden metric of being recommended or not recommended. Sentiment is a bigger part of your life than you probably want it to be.

You also need log file analysis to see whether LLMs are coming to your site . Are there what I call dead zones, where LLMs don’t come back? You think the content is great, but are they fetching it and chunking it back out to the user?

You need all of that measurement. It’s very different from measuring rankings and backlinks.

In the old world, we wanted more rankings, more traffic, and more backlinks. In the AI world, we want more positive citations and recommendations. But we also have to pay much more attention to the negative side.

In SEO, you had to pay attention to the negative when it was in everyone’s face. Someone Googles your brand, your CEO has done something stupid, and you need brand reputation management.

Now that’s part of the suite. You have to learn how to do it and work with those other teams. It can’t be a one-person show anymore.

AEO is much more of a team sport, and it has the ability to be one because leadership cares now. Get that process in place. People within companies have the opportunity to have those conversations.

This is in The Wall Street Journal now. My brother called me one day and asked, “What’s up with this AI thing?”

I said, “Why are you calling me about that? You work at a bank.”

He said, “It’s in the Journal.” I said, “We’ve made it.”

That’s how people have to start thinking. The biggest divergence is measurement, not necessarily the tasks. The tasks need to happen at a greater pace, but the measurement is vastly different.

You have five directions you can go instead of two or three, in my opinion.

### Why AI Search Has The C-Suite’s Attention

Loren: I’ve never considered SEO to be direct response marketing, but I feel we were sometimes held to direct response standards. Other marketing channels were treated more as multiple touchpoints, with multi-touch attribution.

With AI, we know this is part of the consumer’s decision-making journey. AI visibility and valid information in the conversation can help a conversion coming from an email campaign, paid media, or CTV.

If your client is on something like “The View,” nobody’s going to that little URL at the bottom of the screen. They’re typing something in, and now they’re asking ChatGPT about it. It’s part of that whole flow.

I also like your point about AI’s importance within the company. This isn’t coming from the search team, up through digital marketing, the VP of marketing, the CMO, and then the C-suite. It’s coming from the C-suite down.

Patrick: It’s going the other way, which is new for us. I’ve talked to more C-level executives in the last six months than I have in the last six years.

It used to be hard to get those meetings unless you had someone who really understood organic. I think this is healthy for an industry that was getting a little stale.

There were shifts here and there. Google would update the algorithm, and we’d have the same conversation again.

I always joke that we’ve had a lot of meteors come for our jobs. They never quite took us out, but they changed the landscape around us, usually for the better in my opinion.

Even though this is one of the scariest changes for a lot of people, I think it’s one of the best things that’s happened to the industry. That will play out over the next five to 10 years.

We’ll look back and say, “That was one of the best things that happened to me in my career.” Not just for productivity, but because it made me more important in my organization. I had a conversation or opportunity because AI blew up what was going on.

Loren: It’s also the best way to get a task to the top of the dev ticket pile.

One company I’ve been working with did a Shopify relaunch and never set up its canonical tags. I’d been pushing forever. Every week, the update was, “The dev team’s on it.”

Then I realized their AI Overviews visibility had dropped because it was cycling from one version of a URL to another. Eventually, it dropped entirely.

I put in a new ticket: “911 emergency AI issue.”

The thing was fixed in two days. It’s amazing.

Patrick: Then it gets fixed.

### How Brands Earn AI Citations

Loren: Speaking of developing and distributing content, what helps that content earn AI citations?

Patrick: A lot of it is the brand itself. This isn’t a domain authority exercise like traditional search was. It’s a brand authority exercise.

Going back to Reddit’s drop in citation share, that’s not surprising to me. It wasn’t going to stay that high forever as brands figured this out and created more content.

But it’s still part of the consideration phase. When ChatGPT is looking for an answer, it’s still looking at Reddit. It may not cite it or bring content back from there, but it’s looking at the conversation as an authenticity signal, for lack of better words.

In our data, the number one most-cited source is YouTube. It’s a highly trusted community where people have a lot of conversations, and many brands aren’t there in the way they should be .

If you have a well-put-together YouTube presence with playlists, a podcast, webinars, and conference videos, that can help. If it garners enough conversation and clout, those are sites LLMs go to at some point when looking for an answer.

Influencers are there. People trust influencers and expertise.

That’s why one of the greatest opportunities on a site is adding authors. So many sites still don’t have authors or use author schema and article schema. It drives me nuts.

I understand bigger brands saying, “Greg doesn’t want his face on the website.” Then put Tim’s face on the website.

Every brand needs someone out there talking, and you have to find people who are okay being on camera.

At Conductor, we interact with that in different ways. I’m on LinkedIn, I’ve been doing my webinar, 30|30, for 11 years, and I’ve spoken at a million conferences.

Seth loves LinkedIn and posts all the time. Our head of marketing, Lindsay, does the same.

It’s not necessarily about the name. There are people at Conductor who LLMs can establish are experts in the space, based on the longevity of their careers and whether they’ve contributed to the industry.

I do believe they go to LinkedIn and somewhat credentialize people. But it’s also, “Have they contributed to the industry I’m looking for this answer in?”

That’s easy for LLMs to figure out. For traditional search engines, it wasn’t so straightforward.

I always say search engines live in a 2D world. There’s an index, and they go up and down in it.

LLMs live in a 3D world, looking all over the place and picking things from across the universe. Those conversations are happening in communities, on sites like Quora , and on YouTube.

If you look at the top 15% of all the sites in our data, seven of them are social sites. Facebook, LinkedIn, YouTube at number one, and Reddit are there.

Why? It’s real people having real conversations. That’s what the LLM is looking for.

### Where To Invest In Your 2027 AEO Strategy

Loren: It’s October, Thanksgiving stuff is already in the store, and Halloween isn’t here yet. Next thing you know, it’ll be the holidays and New Year’s.

If I’m planning my company’s AI and AEO strategy for 2027, where do I begin? What should I invest in first, and how do I pregame?

Patrick: Invest in your expertise first. Understand who the people in your organization are that you can tie content back to, and then tie that content back to them.

Create author pages and use author schema and article schema. Invest in your people.

Find someone you know is good on camera but may be nervous to raise their hand, and push them to do it. Flaunt your expertise.

Number two, plan to make more video. Start a podcast. Someone in your company is yearning to do this right now, so give them the opportunity.

Learn how to do it. It might suck at first, and that’s okay. You’ll get better over time.

Participate in more webinars. That’s another way to amplify your expertise.

Number three, fix your process. I’m sure people listening are thinking, “Man, my company’s process stinks.” Most companies’ processes stink.

People are working on smoothing those out so they can build a flywheel process. That allows you to experiment more, create more content, and optimize more content.

Buy software that helps you track it more easily, whether that’s Conductor or something else. Go out and look around. There’s a lot of great technology out there.

I think we’re the best, but of course I do.

If you do those three things, the last one is the most important. The flywheel process lets you move faster and do more. You’ll be more productive, learn more, and win faster because everyone’s lined up.

When AI throws something at you, at least you have a system where you can go to the social team and they’re expecting you to give them something to do. You don’t have to fight a six-month battle.

That’s how I think people should operate where we stand today. Will it change in six months? Probably, but I don’t think the tactics will be vastly different.

Loren: It reminds me of those reels about disappearing for a month, learning AI tools, and coming back on top of your game.

Patrick: Just go and do stuff.

Loren: My kid has been playing Roblox for years. He asked, “Can I download Claude Desktop, hook it up to Roblox Studio, and make some games?”

Next thing I know, it’s 11 o’clock on a school night. Don’t judge me. He’s sitting at his computer doing the same thing I’m doing for work, just at a different level.

Watching him gave me ideas. I thought, “Why am I logging into all this stuff? I should be using Claude Desktop for all of it.”

Patrick: It’s the coolest thing that your son can do that. I assume he doesn’t know how to code, or at least not as well as a developer.

Loren: He can barely even draw. He didn’t inherit his father’s artistic talents.

But he’s creative. Seeing that creativity find a different canvas gave me ideas. There are a lot of ways we can implement this throughout our lives.

Patrick: The best way is just to do it. I see so many companies and people stuck in analysis paralysis, asking, “What should I do?”

Something. Just start learning.

It took me a minute when this started, but I feel pretty good about where I am. I still have eons to learn about using this technology.

It’s cool that your son has that ability. I’m going to tell my son to do that instead of playing Roblox, which I’m sure he’s doing right now. Half my salary goes to Robux.

Loren: Pat, it’s been a pleasure hosting you. I like our conversations in this format. It’s fun.

Patrick: Loren, it’s always good chatting with you. Thanks for having me on.

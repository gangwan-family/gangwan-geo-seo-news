The instinct is usually to promise suppression and start building content. Sometimes that is correct. Often there is a faster path that nobody checked for, and occasionally the content is legally removable and the client spent a year burying it instead.

### Start Here: Which Type Of Removal Does Your Content Qualify For?

Three different outcomes get called “removal,” and conflating them is where most of the wasted effort comes from.

- Removal: The source page is gone. The publisher deleted it, the platform took it down, or a court ordered it. This is the only outcome that also stops the content from feeding AI answers, which is why it matters more now than it did five years ago.

- Deindexing: The page still exists but Google no longer returns it. The URL is live for anyone with the link, and crawlers and scrapers can still reach it.

- Suppression : The page exists, remains indexed, and you have pushed it below the visible fold with other content. Nothing was removed. You have changed what people see first.

Suppression is still a legitimate and often necessary strategy. It is simply the weakest of the three against AI surfaces, because a model trained on or retrieving from a corpus does not care what position the source held. Pew Research Center’s browsing data analysis found that when an AI summary appears, users click a traditional search result only 8% of the time, roughly half the rate of searches without one. When the summary is the answer, position ten and position three can carry the same weight.

Our own demand data reflects the shift. At Erase.com, inbound requests specifically to address negative AI Overview and assistant-answer content are up roughly 215% year over year, and a growing share of clients now ask for help with long-tail branded queries like a name plus “reddit” or a brand plus “reviews,” because those are the question-style searches most likely to trigger an AI summary. Three years ago, most of our clients only cared about their name by itself.

#### 3 Questions To Ask First

Run every negative URL through three questions before you spend a cent.

- Is the page still live, or already gone?

- Can you point to a specific factual error, not just an unflattering fact?

- Is there a court order, dismissal, or expungement attached to it?

A “yes” on the second or third question usually means removal is worth pursuing. A “no” across the board, especially on a legitimate news or government site, usually means suppression is your realistic path. Write the answer next to each URL so you’re not re-arguing the same case months from now.

Key Takeaway: Before you commit budget, decide which of the three outcomes the content actually qualifies for. Most wasted spend in reputation work comes from running the wrong play.

### How To Remove A Negative News Article From Google Search Results

Usually not through Google. Google will not deindex a legitimate news article on request, and asking is not a strategy.

The realistic paths, in order of how often they work:

- Factual correction: If the article contains a demonstrable factual error, most reputable outlets have a corrections process. This does not remove the article, but a corrected article often reframes the claim that was actually damaging.

- Unpublishing requests: Some outlets, particularly local and regional publications, will unpublish older articles about non-public figures, especially where charges were dropped or the matter resolved. This has become a formal movement in journalism: NPR has documented how The Boston Globe’s “Fresh Start” initiative and Cleveland.com’s “Right to Be Forgotten” program let people petition to have old stories updated, anonymized, or delisted. Coverage varies enormously by outlet, and it is worth checking before assuming no.

- Legal action for defamation: Viable where the content is false and provably damaging, and slow and expensive where it is merely unflattering. A court order is the one instrument that reliably produces both removal and deindexing. This is a decision to make with a qualified attorney, not on your own.

- Syndication cleanup: One article frequently appears across a dozen syndication partners, aggregators, and scrapers. Removing the original and leaving the copies accomplishes very little, and the copies are frequently what AI systems cite.

One procedural detail worth knowing: even after a page comes down, Google can keep showing the cached listing and snippet for weeks. Google’s free Outdated Content Tool exists for exactly this gap, and this step-by-step walkthrough of the Outdated Content Tool covers how to submit a refresh request and what gets requests denied.

#### Start With These Two Steps

- Find the outlet’s actual corrections or editorial standards page, not a general contact form, and lay out the factual error with dates, names, and any documents that back up your version.

- Once the article is down or corrected, run Google’s Outdated Content Tool yourself so the cached snippet catches up, instead of waiting on the crawler.

Most outlets will review a clear correction request, even without promising a timeline. Just remember this only clears the original source. Any syndicated copies still need their own request.

Common Mistake: Celebrating when the original article comes down while a dozen syndicated copies keep circulating. The copies are often the ones AI answers pull from.

### How To Remove Court Records & Mugshots From Google

This category is more removable than most people assume, and the law has moved.

- Mugshots: The clearest case. Many states now have statutes making it illegal for mugshot websites to charge removal fees , as legal publisher Nolo outlines, and both Google and the major payment processors have taken action against pay-to-remove mugshot sites. Where charges were dropped, dismissed, or expunged, removal requests to these sites have real leverage, and the sites know it.

- Sealed or expunged records: These should not be published at all. Where a record has been legally expunged and a site is still displaying it, you have an actual claim rather than a request. Verify the expungement is complete first, because the paperwork frequently is not.

- Public court records that remain public: The hard case. If the record is legitimately public and current, removal is unlikely and suppression is the working strategy. Even here, aggregator sites reproducing the record often have their own removal processes separate from the court.

#### Check Your State’s Law First

Search “[your state] mugshot removal law” before contacting any site. A number of states now require free removal once charges are dropped, dismissed, or expunged, and some ban removal fees outright.

Pull the official dismissal paperwork or expungement certificate before you submit a request. A request backed by that document is much harder for a site to ignore than a request alone.

If the record is still legitimately public, don’t wait on the outcome. Start building a more robust digital footprint with a LinkedIn page or simple bio page in parallel since that groundwork helps with suppression either way.

Tip: Sequence matters. Expunge or seal first where possible, then pursue removal. Doing it in the other order means re-litigating every request.

One encouraging trend from our side of the desk: mugshot and gripe-site removal requests at Erase.com have fallen by more than half since 2023. Google’s sustained crackdown has pushed these sites out of visible results, and LLMs largely ignore content that doesn’t rank on Google, Bing, or Brave Search. When a category stops surfacing, the problem shrinks on its own, which is exactly the dynamic that makes AI surfaces the new front to watch.

### How To Remove Personal Information From Data Brokers

Home addresses, phone numbers, relatives, and property records surface through data brokers, and this is the most systematically solvable category on the list. The scale is documented: more than 500 data brokers are registered on the California Privacy Protection Agency’s public data broker registry alone, and under the state’s Delete Act, California residents can now use the DROP platform to send a single deletion request to all of them, with brokers required to begin processing those requests in August 2026.

Nearly every major broker maintains an opt-out process. They are tedious, inconsistently honored, and frequently reversed when the broker refreshes its dataset from public sources, which is why one-time opt-outs fail. The work is real, but it is procedural rather than adversarial.

Two things make this category different from the others:

- Volume: The number of sites involved runs into the hundreds, not a handful.

- Re-listing: Reappearance is the norm rather than the exception. Treat opt-outs as maintenance, not a project.

#### Two Free Tools Worth Using

- Google’s Results About You : monitors your name, address, and phone number in search results. A 2026 update also lets it catch exposed government ID numbers, like a driver’s license or Social Security number.

- California’s DROP platform : sends one deletion request to every registered broker at once. Brokers are required to process those requests as of August 1, 2026.

Either way, treat this as ongoing maintenance. Unfortunately, listings commonly reappear once a broker refreshes its data.

### Can You Remove Reddit & Forum Threads?

Usually not, and they are increasingly consequential.

Reddit will not remove a thread for being unflattering. Moderators occasionally will where a post violates subreddit rules, and posts that contain personal information or clear harassment can be actioned through platform policy.

What has changed is downstream reach. A thread with a few dozen upvotes that never cracks page one now gets scraped into aggregators, quoted in roundup posts, and pulled into retrieval when someone asks an assistant about the brand. The thread itself may be invisible in search while the claim inside it reaches AI answers.

This is now the fastest-growing category of work we see. Requests to address threads that Google features in its “Discussions and forums” module on branded searches, which are the same threads LLMs disproportionately cite, have nearly tripled at Erase.com over the past 18 months. When Google elevates a thread into that module, it stops being one result among ten and becomes the answer, both on the SERP and in the AI systems that draw from it.

#### Report It, Then Go To The Moderators

Start with Reddit’s in-app report option, and pick the personal-information or harassment category only when the post genuinely qualifies. Picking the wrong one just slows things down.

If the report alone doesn’t move it, message the subreddit’s moderators directly through modmail. Name the exact rule the post breaks and keep it short and factual; moderators respond faster to a specific policy citation than a general complaint.

Before spending more time on the original post, check whether it’s already been quoted or screenshotted elsewhere. If it has, the original thread may not be your real problem.

Reality Check: If the citations are all downstream copies, going after the original thread accomplishes nothing. The copies are the work.

### How To Verify A Removal Actually Worked

The old verification was a rank check. That no longer tells you what you need to know.

A page can be removed and deindexed while the claim inside it continues appearing in AI Overviews and assistant answers, carried by copies you never found or absorbed into a model before the takedown landed. Pew Research found that roughly one in five Google searches already triggers an AI summary, and that share climbs to 60% for question-style queries, which is exactly how people search a business’s name plus “reviews” or “scam.”

A workable check after any removal:

- Run the queries: Test the branded queries that surfaced the issue against ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews, not just Google Search.

- Record the citations: Note every URL cited in those answers. Cited sources are the working set actually shaping the narrative.

- Compare against your removals: Anything remaining is unfinished work with a specific address.

- Repeat weekly: Outputs vary run to run, so one clean check is one sample rather than a result.

That citation list is the most useful artifact in modern reputation work, because it converts a vague problem into a finite list of URLs ranked by influence.

### Content Removal FAQs

#### How long does content removal usually take?

It depends on the path. Data broker opt-outs often process in days to weeks, but re-listing means the work recurs. Publisher unpublishing decisions can take weeks. Legal routes, including expungement followed by removal requests, are typically measured in months. Suppression campaigns generally need several months of sustained work before results hold.

#### Should I try this myself or hire a service?

Data broker opt-outs and platform policy reports are reasonable DIY tasks if you have the time and can keep up with re-listings. Business.com’s DIY guide to removing negative content from Google walks through every free Google tool and which category of content each one covers. Matters involving expungements, defamation, or content that has syndicated widely are harder to run alone, and a mistake in sequencing can cost you leverage. Compare a few providers, ask exactly which of the three outcomes they are promising, and get it in writing.

#### Will removed content come back?

Sometimes. Data broker listings frequently reappear when datasets refresh, and removed articles can persist through syndicated copies. That is why verification and ongoing monitoring matter as much as the initial removal.

#### Is suppression still worth doing?

Yes, when the content genuinely cannot be removed or deindexed. It remains the working strategy for legitimate public records and truthful but unflattering coverage. Just go in knowing it is the weakest option against AI answer surfaces, and pair it with syndication cleanup where you can.

### When The Category Is Unclear

The through line across all five types is that the first question is diagnostic. Is this removable, deindexable, or only suppressible? Most wasted budget in reputation work comes from running a suppression campaign against content that had a removal path, or attempting removal on content that never had one.

Where a matter is legally complex, where an expungement is in play, or where content has already syndicated widely enough that the copies outnumber the original, our team at Erase.com handles these as distinct workflows rather than one generic takedown process. But the diagnosis above stands on its own, and running it before committing budget is worth doing regardless of who executes.

Your next three steps: list every negative URL and sort it into one of the three categories, check for syndicated copies of anything you plan to remove, and run your branded queries through the AI surfaces to build your citation list. Get the category right first. The tactics are the easy part.

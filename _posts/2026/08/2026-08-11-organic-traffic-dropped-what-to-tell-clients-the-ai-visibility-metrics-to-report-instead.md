## Organic Traffic Dropped? What to Tell Clients & the AI Visibility Metrics to Report Instead

Organic sessions are down and clients want answers. Here are the four AI search visibility metrics to report instead, and how to track each one.

This post was sponsored by AgencyAnalytics . The opinions expressed in this article are the sponsor’s own.

Organic sessions are down. The client wants to know why.

It’s one of the most common questions of the year, with 42% of agencies saying they’re fielding it. That’s from the 2026 Marketing Agency Benchmarks Report by AgencyAnalytics, based on responses from 494 agency professionals.

A year ago, it came with a clear, short answer. If the content was sound, it was likely a core update, a seasonal dip, or maybe a competitor overtaking the ranking.

In 2026? The answer might be even shorter. Two letters, to be exact: A-I.

### Why Organic Traffic Is Dropping: AI Overviews & Zero-Click Search

An AI Overview answers the search query, so the click never gets made. Or someone asks an AI assistant for a recommendation, follows it, and there’s no reference to where it came from.

It’s not that the ranking shifted or that the content got worse. Nothing on the client’s side went wrong, which makes this drop harder to explain than the ones agencies have been explaining for years.

Most agencies already know this. In the same report, 64% named Google’s AI Overviews as the top industry concern this year, with AI search disrupting traditional SEO close behind at 59%.

The knock-on effects are already showing up: 43% of agencies report greater competition for the high-intent traffic that still converts, and 41% face new challenges measuring return on investment as traditional attribution models lose reliability.

None of this is news to the client either. They’ve probably heard those two letters explain three other numbers this quarter.

### What Clients Actually Want From SEO Reporting

Agencies were also asked which single metric their clients care most about. Conversions came first at 44%, then leads at 22%, ROI at 12%, and revenue at 11%. For 89% of agencies, the answer was an outcome.

Something that may come as a surprise, however, is that traffic came in at a mere 6%. Behind every outcome on the list.

So, why are agencies commonly getting a question about a metric that clients rank so low?

#### Why Attribution Is Getting Harder

Because declining traffic is the first symptom they can see. It sits upstream of everything being tracked, so a drop in sessions is a warning sign: fewer visitors now means fewer conversions next quarter. What clients really want to know is where they stand.

The problem is that many agencies can’t answer that with confidence. Nearly half of agencies say they can’t reliably track people who discover a brand through AI tools. That’s an uncomfortable position to be in as the paid expert. Someone follows a recommendation from an assistant, lands on the site with no referrer attached, and gets logged as direct traffic. The discovery happened, but the record of it didn’t, which leaves the traffic chart as the only number on the table.

It isn’t an isolated gap either. When the survey asked about attribution challenges, the responses came back almost tied:

- 48% struggle to track users from AI discovery.

- 47% can’t attribute conversions across multi-session journeys.

- 45% don’t know which content influenced a conversion.

- 44% say traditional attribution models are losing reliability.

As the report puts it, attribution has become a systemic challenge, and it’s only getting harder as AI-powered discovery creates touchpoints you can’t track.

Which puts more weight on the client report itself. A staggering 97% of agencies rate accurate reporting as important for retention. When traditional models make ROI harder to prove, the report becomes the proof.

### So, What Do You Tell Clients?

#### Stop Defending the Traffic Chart

Instead, show them where they show up in AI. Clients are already asking for it. Demand for answer engine optimization (AEO) and SEO in AI search services hit an all-time high of 66%. That makes it the top new service request of 2026, ahead of even paid ads and short-form video.

#### The 4 AI Search Visibility Metrics Worth Reporting

There are four metrics agencies can measure and report on now that give the client the real insight they’re looking for. AgencyAnalytics built all four into AI Tracker . You can also track them manually. Here’s how:

##### Visibility (Is the Client Showing Up at All?)

Tracking how often the client gets named across a set of prompts their buyers would realistically use. Keep category prompts separate from branded ones, since a model repeating a company name back after someone typed it in isn’t evidence of much.

- Write 20 to 30 prompts real buyers would use, and repeat the set per location if the client serves multiple markets.

- Run them monthly, logged out of the AI platform or in a private session, using the same wording each time (answers may vary, so run each twice).

- Log every mention, then work out what percentage of runs named the client, per engine.

##### Position (How Early Do They Show Up?)

Where the client lands when they’re named. People skim AI answers the way they skim a results page, so those at the top get the most attention.

- When the client is named, record their place in the list and the list length (third out of five is not the same as third out of 12).

- Calculate the average across the answers where they appear, then track the direction month over month.

##### Citations (Are They Surfacing As a Source?)

Every large language model draws from sources, and those sources are usually visible, whether it’s the client’s page or a competitor’s that gets referenced.

- Expand the sources panel and log every domain cited, noting any answer that gives none.

- Tag each one: client, competitor, or third party such as a directory or review site.

- Log the specific URL, not just the domain (this will tell you which page the model is pulling from).

##### Sentiment (What’s the Model Saying About Them?)

How the client gets described in the answer, and whether anything in there needs flagging.

- Copy the sentence describing the client verbatim.

- Tag it positive, neutral, or negative, using the same rule each month.

- Flag factual errors separately, e.g., outdated pricing, dropped services, wrong location (those are quick wins you can fix today).

Tracked over time, those four metrics turn “where do we stand?” into a report you can put in front of the client.

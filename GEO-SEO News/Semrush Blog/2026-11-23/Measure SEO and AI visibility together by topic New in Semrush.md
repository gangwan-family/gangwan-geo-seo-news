## Measure SEO and AI visibility together by topic: New in Semrush

Author : Luke Harsel

8 min read

November 23, 2026

Search used to be simple. It started with a keyword and ended with blue links. Now, people get a shortlist of brands from an AI answer, look up those brands on Google, and switch back and forth with follow-up questions until they decide.

Most SEOs adapting to AI search try to track it one keyword or one prompt at a time, with separate tools and reports for SEO and generative engine optimization (GEO). But that leaves you with two halves of the same buyer journey. More noise and less clarity about what to do next.

Tracking by topic works better. A topic groups together related keywords and prompts so you can see your visibility in search and AI answers in one place. Semrush now supports this topic-first approach in two new tools: Organic Rankings 2.0 and Keyword Magic Tool 2.0.

Use topics to check your own visibility, find new areas to target, and benchmark competitors on the topics you share.

### Why a topic-first approach works

AI visibility is harder to measure than SEO. AI platforms don’t share the kind of first-party data you get from Google Search Console and Google Analytics, and one prompt only estimates a small part of what people might ask an AI tool. Topics give you a wider view and a clearer picture.

When an AI platform gets a prompt that requires search, it fans the prompt out into related sub-queries on the same topic and runs its own searches for each. If you’re visible across those related searches, you’re more likely to be found when the fan-out runs.

Our research found that when a brand owns a whole topic , not just a single prompt, it shows up for more of the related prompts. That’s why SEO and AI visibility correlate strongly at the topic level: the searches an AI runs in the background should line up with where you already compete.

### What’s new: Topics in Organic Rankings 2.0 and Keyword Magic Tool 2.0

To help you work with topics, we’ve released two new tools, updated how we calculate organic traffic, and added one brand new metric.

#### New tools

- Organic Rankings 2.0: See every topic a domain competes in, with SEO and AI visibility metrics for each. The original Organic Rankings tool covers keyword rankings and organic search only. Version 2.0 starts with topics and includes prompts, AI mentions, and citations.

- Keyword Magic Tool 2.0: Run keyword research and prompt research in a single workflow. Keywords are grouped into subtopics by SERP similarity, so your research is organized by topic clusters you can plan content around.

Note: The original Organic Rankings and Keyword Magic Tool remain available alongside the 2.0 versions.

#### Updated and new metrics

- Organic traffic (updated): A new click-through model that accounts for AI Overviews, other SERP features, search intent, and branded vs. non-branded search when estimating organic traffic from keyword positions.

- AI-influenced traffic (new): An estimate of the visits, from any channel, that follow a brand's mentions in AI answers. It's trained on a reference dataset of aggregated web activity that links brand mentions to site visits within 24-hours (including via direct, organic search, or other channels).

Use both metrics directionally, to compare sites and spot changes over time, not as exact visit counts.

### How to use topics to grow your SEO and AI visibility

Here’s the workflow: map the topics you compete in, benchmark competitors, find gaps between your rankings and AI mentions, and dive into target keywords and prompts from subtopics. If you’re starting a topic from scratch, use Keyword Magic Tool 2.0 instead.

#### See every topic where you compete

Start by establishing your baseline of every topic you compete in.

Open Organic Rankings 2.0 and enter your domain to run the report.

Rather than a list of keywords, the tool will display all the topics where you compete. For each one, you can compare:

- Total search volume (combined volume of keywords and prompts)

- AI and organic traffic

- Keywords, mentions and citations

#### Benchmark competitors across your target topics

Topics let you benchmark competitors across search and AI answers at once. Instead of comparing keyword lists and prompt lists separately, you compare your metrics by topic, each with rankings, mentions, and estimated traffic in one row.

Run the same Organic Rankings 2.0 report on a direct competitor to see how their visibility compares with yours, topic by topic.

Start by comparing your shared topics. Find the topics that appear on both your Organic Rankings 2.0 report and theirs, and compare rankings, mentions, and estimated traffic side by side.

Compare both reports and record the metrics to compare as benchmarks in a spreadsheet.

To see where their AI visibility is strongest, sort their topics by estimated AI-influenced traffic . This shows you the categories where their AI visibility influences visits to their site.

Look for topics on their list that aren't on yours. These are topics where they've built visibility and you don't compete yet. If one is relevant for your business, open it and check which subtopics they rank for.

Repeat this process for two or three direct competitors across your market. Topics that appear for every competitor are your core categories. Check these regularly to see how your position changes over time.

#### Find gaps between your rankings and AI mentions

To see which topics drive the most organic visits at the moment, sort by organic traffic. Then sort by AI-influenced traffic to see where your AI visibility is influencing the most visits.

You might notice a different order of topics sorted by these two metrics. Compare your organic traffic to AI-influenced traffic for the same topic.

A large difference between the two estimates points to a gap between your SEO and GEO.

High organic traffic with low AI-influenced traffic shows that you rank well on Google, but AI answers rarely mention your brand for this topic. Take note of what AI answers cite instead, to understand how your content could be stronger.

High AI-influenced traffic with low organic traffic shows AI answers mention your brand more than your rankings would suggest.

That shows you have some authority on the topic, and you have room to win more keyword rankings and organic traffic.

#### Dive into subtopics to grow your topical authority

To explore a particular topic, click on any topic to open the single-topic view. This report shows your trend on the topic and the topic’s keywords and prompts that Semrush groups together.

Start with the trend chart to see whether your presence in this topic is growing, flat, or slipping. This is a quick way to check whether your work on a particular topic moved the needle.

The table shows the topic’s subtopics, with all the keywords and prompts in each.

Select a subtopic to see where you rank for its keywords. This list acts as an SEO roadmap for you to build topical authority around the subject. Each keyword shows your current position, so you can see which ones you already rank for and which ones you’re missing.

From there, apply filters to explore opportunities.

- Low competition: Set a Keyword Difficulty (KD) range that fits your site. Newer sites should start with “Very easy” and “Easy” keywords (KD 0–29). Sites that already rank for related terms can go up to “Possible” (KD 30–49).

- High intent: Filter search intent to commercial and transactional.

- Quick wins: Filter for positions 11–20 to find keywords on page two that are close to breaking onto page one.

Then switch to the Prompts view to find prompts where AI mentions or cites your brand.

Together, the keywords and prompts show the full set of questions your content needs to answer. Because they are grouped by subtopic, one page can cover the search and AI side of the same question.

Click Export above the table to download the list of keywords or prompts as a CSV file. Use the file to save your research and later build content briefs or an optimization plan.

Covering subtopics in this way helps you build topical authority across the wider topic.

#### Research new topics with Keyword Magic Tool 2.0

If your site is brand-new, or you don’t have rankings for a topic or subtopic you want to target, start with Keyword Magic Tool 2.0 .

Enter a seed keyword you want to cover into the tool. The top of the report shows a broad overview of volume and difficulty.

Then scroll to the “Research topic ideas” area of the report to explore the related topics and subtopics. Each subtopic is a smaller group of related keywords and prompts within that topic.

Unlike the original version of the tool, which grouped keywords by shared words, Keyword Magic Tool 2.0 groups them by SERP similarity. Keywords in the same subtopic return many of the same search results, so one page can often target the whole group. Plan each subtopic as part of your topic cluster strategy .

A topic cluster is a set of pages on related subtopics, all linked to a pillar page that covers the broad topic.

Start with the subtopic closest to what you sell, and build content to rank for these keywords and associate your brand with the subject matter. Cover one subtopic at a time to build up your topical authority across the wider topic.

### How to build a topical authority plan for AI search

A topical authority plan helps you grow your visibility across Google and AI search at the same time. At Semrush, we’ve developed a framework called Topical Gravity to help you define topics you want to target and build authority across both.

Here’s how to use it:

- Define your topics: Use Organic Rankings 2.0 to choose the topics you want your brand to own, using your competitor benchmark as a starting point.

- Build content around each priority subtopic: Use the keywords and prompts you exported to brief pages that answer the full set of keywords, prompts, and questions in a subtopic, not one query at a time.

- Find the sources AI cites: Use the Visibility Overview to see which sources AI answers cite for your topic. These could be review platforms, industry publications, or communities. Targeting these for mentions and links can build your authority.

- Track progress by topic: Return to Organic Rankings 2.0 regularly to see how your brand visibility for each topic is moving. When leadership asks whether visibility is up, down, or flat, you'll have a topic-level answer, and you can open the topic to see which keywords and prompts have changed.

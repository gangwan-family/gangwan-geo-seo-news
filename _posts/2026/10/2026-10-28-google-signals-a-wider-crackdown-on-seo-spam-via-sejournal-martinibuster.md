## Google Signals A Wider Crackdown On SEO Spam

Google expands its spam guidelines to address a new category of UGC SEO spam, signaling a broader direction for manual actions.

Google takes aim at a new source of SEO spam, signaling that platforms, tools, and services may face consequences for content published by their users. It’s also a warning to SEOs and website owners who leverage these kinds of sites for links and AI search influence.

The three changes are:

- Adds sharing platforms as a type of site that can be penalized for UGC spam.

- Provides steps that platforms can take to prevent UGC Spam

- Advises on how to become aware of harmful activities and manual actions

All three advisories are specific to platforms which means that Google may take actions against these kinds of sites. SEOs who are promoting sites with these kinds of sites should become aware of these policies and the platform owners should also be aware of these new guidelines.

### Change 1: Spam Advisory Addresses Sharing Services

The first change is the addition of a few words that add these sharing platforms as the kinds of sites that the guidelines apply to.

These are the new words added to the page:

- “…and tools that let users publish shared pages, generated content, or web apps…”

- “pages or”

This is the full context, with the newly added words in italics:

“Spammers often take advantage of open comment forms and other user generated content inputs and generate spammy content on an unsuspecting victim site. Hosting platforms and tools that let users publish shared pages, generated content, or web apps may be similarly open to abuse; spammers may create a large number of pages or sites that violate our spam policies and add little or no value to the web.”

#### Takeaway for SEOs and Platform Owners

Google may be planning to target tool and app platforms that enable users to generate content. SEOs should add this to their site audits to check if clients are using these kinds of sites in manipulative ways. Platform owners should take notice and make necessary changes as outlined in the next sections.

Google may in the near future crack down on tool and app platforms that enable users to generate content on the web pages of those platforms.

### Change 2: Defending Against UGC Spam

The second change to the spam support page explains what platform owners should do in order to prevent harmful behavior on their platforms that could result in a manual action by Google (removal from the search results).

Google added an entirely new section that is targeted at platforms or websites that allow user generated content. The new guidelines outline steps the platforms should take to avoid receiving a manual action.

These steps are specific to platforms that enable users to generate web pages. This also includes services that are meant for content sharing or collaboration.

Typical platforms:

- Services

- Tools

- Sites

- Apps

The new section advises users of these kinds of sites and platforms to use the no-index directive on pages that are not meant to be indexed by search engines. For example, use a no-index rule in either the robots.txt or with a meta no-index tag if the function of a service is to generate shareable pages or tools but are not meant to be indexed.

The new guidelines advise:

“Only index content meant for Search

If your service lets users create shareable pages, generated outputs, or interactive tools, consider whether that content is meant to be found through search engines or only accessed directly by people with a link.

When pages are created for direct sharing, collaboration, or utility rather than public search discovery, apply the noindex rule (using a robots meta tag or X-Robots-Tag HTTP response header) or block crawling in robots.txt by default.”

### Change 3: Preparation For Manual Action

The third and last change to Google’s spam page is an explanation of how to track and recognize the signs that users are causing potentially negative changes to a a website and how to know when a site has reached a point where Google has taken action against it.

This is the new section added to the website:

“Verify your site or subdomain in Search Console so you receive email alerts if Google issues a manual action against your site or detects security issues. Also check the Page Indexing report and Search performance report regularly for unexpected spikes in indexed pages, impressions, or queries that don’t match your platform’s purpose.

Track platform-level creation and indexing metrics, and set up alerts for anomalies such as spikes in newly published public pages or a small number of users generating a large share of indexable URLs.”

### Takeaways

Google Expands Its UGC Spam Guidelines

- Google’s updated policies now explicitly address platforms that enable users to publish shared pages, AI-generated content, and web apps, extending the guidance beyond traditional user-generated content.

Platforms Face Greater Responsibility For User-Generated Spam

- Google’s changes put platform owners on notice that allowing users to publish spam can expose the entire platform to search penalties.

Google Draws A Distinction Between Shareable Content And Indexable Content

- Not everything users create is intended for search engines. Google advises platforms to prevent indexing of content intended primarily for sharing, collaboration, or utility.

Spam Prevention Becomes A Platform-Level Responsibility

- Google’s updated guidance emphasizes proactive prevention, monitoring, and early detection of abusive publishing activity rather than waiting for a manual action.

Implications For SEO Audits

- SEOs should consider whether clients use third-party publishing platforms in ways that violate Google’s spam policies and whether the platforms themselves are vulnerable to abuse.

Potential For Expanded Enforcement

- The changes suggest Google may be paying closer attention to spam originating from platforms that enable users to publish generated content, although the updated guidance does not establish that a crackdown is imminent.

Google updated its user-generated spam guidelines to explicitly address platforms that enable users to publish shared pages, generated content, and web apps, putting platform owners on notice that they may face search penalties for spam published by their users. The changes suggest Google is taking a closer look at how these platforms are being abused and may be preparing for greater enforcement.

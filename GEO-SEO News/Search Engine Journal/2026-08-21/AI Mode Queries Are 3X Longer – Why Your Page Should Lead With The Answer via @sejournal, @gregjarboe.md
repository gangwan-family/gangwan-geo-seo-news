## AI Mode Queries Are 3X Longer – Why Your Page Should Lead With The Answer

One year of AI Mode data shows people asking questions, not typing fragments. Why the lead-first paragraph now beats the narrative arc.

SEO practitioners who rely on long-form narrative arcs to keep users on a page are losing ground to automated search synthesis. Google AI Overviews compress multi-source information into immediate factual summaries, requiring content architects to prioritize immediate data retrieval over traditional brand storytelling. If your pages bury the primary answer, you make it harder for the systems that assemble AI Mode responses to pull a usable passage .

How can you verify this for yourself? Start by reading “ How AI Mode Is Changing The Way People Search In The U.S. ,” which was written by Shivani Mohan, Google’s vice president, Data Science and UXR, and published on the Google blog on May 19, 2026.

### The Query Itself Has Changed Shape

AI Mode has passed 1 billion monthly active users globally , and queries have more than doubled every quarter since launch. But the volume number is the least interesting part of this story. What’s more interesting is that people aren’t just searching more frequently; they’re asking questions differently. The average AI Mode query in the U.S. is triple the length of a traditional search query, and more than one in six AI Mode searches now include something other than text, whether that’s an image, voice, or a live back-and-forth conversation. Image-based queries are growing more than 40% month over month.

Follow-up questions are climbing at the same rate. People start a search, then keep going, refining and narrowing in a way that looks nothing like the one-shot keyword query that SEO strategy has been built around for two decades. Google’s own list of the most common first words in AI Mode queries confirms it. “ What ,” “ how ,” “ I ,” “ is ,” and “ can ” now lead the pack, not short-head nouns. People are asking questions the way they’d ask another person, not typing fragments the way they’d type into a search box.

### 5 Verbs Replaced The Keyword

Mohan’s report groups the new behavior into five modes: Explore , Decide , Learn , Create , and Do . Each one maps to a distinct kind of content need, and each is growing at a different rate.

Explore queries, the open-ended, brainstorming kind, are growing 30% faster than AI Mode traffic overall. Decide queries built around comparison words like “which of” and “which one” are growing 40% faster. Learn queries help people understand new concepts and explore professional development opportunities. Do queries tied to planning, from workout routines to travel itineraries to household budgets, are growing 80% faster than AI Mode queries overall over the past six months. Image creation queries in AI Mode have more than tripled since the start of the year.

None of these maps cleanly onto a keyword. They map onto a task. That’s the shift, and it’s the one that clearly indicates we’re not on a relatively shorter, straightforward journey to plot a course around ranking-factor changes. We’re on a significantly longer, more complicated odyssey to navigate user-behavior transformation.

### What This Means For Your Content Strategy

If you’re still building content briefs around a head term and related keywords, this data says that model is aging out in real time, not in some hypothetical AI-search future. So, how should you change your content strategy this quarter?

Follow the lead of journalists, who traditionally wrote stories chronologically – until a new technology, the telegraph, changed their writing style in the 19th century.

The transition to a lead-first structure became an industry standard between 1880 and 1890 due to simple economics and mechanical limits. The Associated Press paid telegraph operators by the word, making poetic preambles an unnecessary tax on profits. In newspaper back shops, editors assembling heavy metal type needed a reliable way to cut stories from the bottom up to fit physical page constraints without removing critical facts.

Today, generative search engines operate under similar computational and mathematical constraints.

I learned this lesson the hard way, decades before Google rolled out AI Overview. In 1986, as the newly minted director of corporate communications at Lotus, I proudly dropped a thick binder of press clippings on CEO Jim Manzi’s desk. He glanced at it and told me that until I could “ measure the impact of PR in cold, hard cash ,” he didn’t want to see my so-called reports. That line sent me down a 40-year rabbit hole into measurement, and it’s the same demand generative search is now making of every writer on the web: Stop burying the value, prove it immediately, or get skipped.

In my judgment, trying to protect a vague brand voice by hiding core facts inside fluffy introductions is a fast track to search irrelevance. You do not need to turn your writing into sterile robot prose, but you must align your site architecture with how algorithmic synthesis processes data.

### 5 Practical Steps For AI-Driven Search Optimization

Here are five concrete steps you can apply to your content strategy today to improve visibility in AI search summaries.

#### 1. Lead With Entity-Dense Opening Sentences

Place your primary definition, key metric, or main conclusion directly in the first sentence of every core section. Anchor your sentences with specific brand names, clear geographic markers, exact dates, and verified numerical values. Avoiding vague generalizations makes your prose mathematically readable for search engines and reduces the risk of AI hallucination.

#### 2. Implement Scannable Hybrid Layouts

Organize body copy into short paragraphs that average two or three sentences. Follow major section headers with concise summaries, ordered lists, or structured tables . This approach allows search crawlers to extract clean segments for generated overviews while giving human readers a frictionless scanning experience.

#### 3. Write For The Follow-Up, Not Just The First Click

If more than 40% month-over-month growth is happening in multi-turn conversations, your content needs to answer the second and third question a reader would ask after the one your title promised. Structure long-form content so a section can stand on its own as the answer to a specific follow-up, because that’s the chunk AI Mode is most likely to surface.

#### 4. Build Content Around The 5 Verbs, Not 5 Keywords

Before you brief a piece, ask whether it’s meant to help someone explore , decide , learn , create , or do something. A comparison page written for “which” queries needs a genuinely different structure than a guide written for “how to get started” queries, even if the topic and the target keyword look similar on paper.

#### 5. Treat Image And Multimodal Content As A Ranking Input, Not An Afterthought

With image queries growing 40% month over month and AI Mode’s Nano Banana integration driving a tripling of image-creation queries since January, alt text, image context, and visual content quality are no longer secondary to the words on the page. They’re part of what AI Mode is reading.

### Final Takeaway

It’s taken me 40 years to reach this conclusion: SEO success in the current search era is not about flooding the web with generic automated drafts. It is about mastering structural clarity so both search algorithms and human readers receive immediate, verified utility.

Back in the 19th century, journalists had to change their writing style when a new technology, the telegraph, forced them to change the way they told stories. Now, in the 21st century, SEOs need to change our writing style as AI Overviews and AI Mode force us to rethink our content strategies.
